# Nissim Farhy — Research Portfolio

Physics & Applied Mathematics student at Yeshiva University.

My research interests include **computational physics, applied mathematics, complex systems, network science, stochastic processes, and machine learning**. This repository collects selected research projects, ongoing work, and computational investigations.

> **Note:** Several projects listed here are ongoing and contain preliminary results. Project pages distinguish between ongoing research, thesis work, conference-presented research, and coursework.

---

## Featured Research

### [Overload Cascades in Spatial Networks](projects/cascades.md)

**Ongoing Research**

Computational study of cascading failures in spatial lattice networks, with emphasis on how attack location, boundary effects, network tolerance, and shortest-path betweenness influence cascade propagation and the emergence of secondary failure waves.

**Methods:** Network science · Graph theory · Betweenness centrality · Computational simulation

---

### [Gaussian Regularization and Topological Structure](projects/sigreg-topology.md)

**Ongoing Research**

Investigation of the interaction between Gaussian latent-space regularization and preservation of nontrivial manifold topology in learned representations, using toroidal data and geometric and topological diagnostics.

**Methods:** Representation learning · Persistent homology · Manifold geometry · Machine learning

---

### [Self-Exciting Dynamics in Automated Financial Markets](projects/hawkes-amm.md)

**Undergraduate Honors Thesis — Work in Progress**

Study of temporal clustering and cross-excitation among large trades and liquidity-management events in automated financial markets using multivariate Hawkes processes.

**Methods:** Stochastic processes · Hawkes processes · Maximum-likelihood estimation · High-frequency financial data

---

### [Network Analysis of Wikipedia Writers](projects/wikipedia-networks.md)

**Research Project — Presented at NetSci and Yeshiva University**

Network-science investigation of relationships among writers and their works using Wikipedia-derived data, with comparative structural and spectral analysis of the resulting networks.

**Methods:** Network science · Spectral analysis · Graph metrics · Computational data analysis
